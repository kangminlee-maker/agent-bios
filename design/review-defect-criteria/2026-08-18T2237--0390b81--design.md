# Design — the defect-criterion catalog: what ships, where it lives, how the enum binds

**Status: proposal. Nothing here edits the corpus; the user's approval gates step 2 of the
build order.** This record was revised during its own PR review (the "amended per PR
review" clauses); it was never merged under an earlier label, so this label carries the
time the whole content was true and supersedes nothing. Synthesized from two independent frontier drafts off one blind packet —
Claude (fable-5) and GPT (gpt-5.6-sol, ultra) — per the dual-provider design rule. The
packet, both raw drafts, and the source document are in the session scratchpad; the frame is
`2026-08-16T0110--560ed35--backlog.md` and the source is the peer repo's
`docs/review-defect-criteria.md` (39-round measured evidence).

**Where the providers split, and what was taken.** The drafts disagreed on the core: Claude
proposed a guide with enforcement at each route's accepting channel and the launcher
deferred; GPT proposed a machine-readable catalog plus a shipped runtime that compiles
criterion-specific result schemas, versioned request/result contracts, receipt-chain
integration, and no prose fallback (routes that cannot enforce a schema become advisory and
cannot earn credit). The synthesis takes **Claude's v1 shape** — the corpus's own inert-value
rule decides it: a catalog file and runtime with no consumer today is a second copy, not a
channel, and GPT's no-prose-fallback would demote both shipped deep-review methods
(codex-exec, ultracode) to advisory, a product regression v1 must not smuggle in. **GPT's
structural stack survives as the deferred stage's design seed** (§7 step 6): if/when the
launcher gains the criterion slot, GPT's contract sketch (compiled enum schema, request/
result digests bound into receipts, adapters declaring which output contracts they enforce)
is the shape to build, and its insistence that "an enum in prose drifts from an enum in a
schema" becomes true at that moment. GPT also contributed the sharper golden-admission
audit and the evidence-boundary corrections; both are folded in below.

## 1. Home — a new deployed guide *(Claude; GPT's hybrid rejected for v1, revisit at stage 6)*

`claude/guides/review-defect-criteria.md`, English (AI-consumed corpus is English-unified,

Carries: the principle (declare the criterion before the review starts), the criterion
schema (§3), the four catalog entries (§3, with the golden-bar caveat), the pre-review
procedure, and the golden lifecycle (§4). Routing is guide-to-guide:

- `coding-staged-workflow.md` Review Loop — one sentence: "material issues reach zero" is

- No `claude/CLAUDE.md` change: the Verification Discipline router bullet already fires at
  the moment of composing a review request; the failure mode is not recognition failure
  (AGENTS.md §8's test), so no global bullet.

**Rejected.** (b) A section in `coding-staged-workflow.md`: loads for all development work;

## 2. The term — **defect criterion** *(both drafts converged)*

"The declared, per-review definition of defect for a system type × work goal: observer,
defect, evidence bar, non-defects, classification enum, stop condition, misclassification
cost, goldens." Nearest neighbors, per the concept-economy obligation: the **severity
ladder** answers *how bad*, never *whether* — the source's plateau (17→23) happened entirely
inside "material"; **deep review** names mechanisms, and a criterion is what any mechanism
is handed; **consumption surface** names where it reaches a model. Enters
`ontology/instances/graph.json` §lexicon (bound: the guide path + `guide_id`); `LEXICON.md`
re-emitted, never hand-edited. "Golden" stays a schema field, not a lexicon row. The
ordering both drafts agreed on: system type + goal → **defect criterion** → classification
→ defect? → severity → materiality → stop evaluation.

## 3. The catalog schema and the four entries *(Claude's prose-with-structure; GPT's audit)*

Per criterion: `observer · defect · classes (the enum, ONE class marked stop-relevant) ·
evidence (what one finding must present) · non_defects · stop_condition (what "0" means,
counted over the stop class, and WHY it is reachable — how a growing surface is pinned) ·
misclassification_cost (which error is expensive) · goldens (≥2+, ≥2−, ≥1 boundary; each
with why + date + provenance)`. An entry with an empty cell is not a criterion. The four
entries ship — web service/API, library/SDK, AI harness, decision ontology — with GPT's
admission audit applied: **library/SDK and AI-harness currently carry one negative golden
each, below the bar; the ontology entries need self-contained fixtures.** The build fills
them — and the **AI-harness entry is mandatory for v1**, because §6 binds this repo's next
harness packet to it and a criterion the shipped guide lacks may not be declared (amended
per PR review); its goldens come from this repo's own loop ("a guard satisfied by an
absence", the known-opposite instrument test). The other three ship only if they clear the
bar; "ship fewer" drops an entry AND every migration sentence that names it, and the bar is
never weakened to admit an entry.

## 4. Golden lifecycle *(Claude's, with GPT's evidence labels)*

- **Admission**: only a golden that decides a boundary the definition leaves open, evidenced
  by a recorded classification disagreement or audited misclassification; provenance
  labeled `measured` (dated incident) or `constructed` (authored to pin a boundary).
- **Overturn**: only by a measured counterexample; the change is in-place in the guide
  (active docs describe the present) plus a decision record naming the closed golden and
  the counterexample, plus re-emitted mirrors. Silent deletion forbidden. *(GPT's
  alternative — immutable revisions with superseded entries kept readable — is the right
  shape for the machine catalog at stage 6; for a guide, dated decision records are this
  repo's existing history channel.)*

## 5. Enum enforcement, ranked by channel *(Claude; GPT seeds stage 6)*

1. **Routes with a submit schema**: classification is a required enum field — the measured
   precedent is anchors (2,466/2,466 because the schema refuses output without them).
2. **Prose-packet routes** (codex-exec, ultracode — the shipped methods): the packet header
   declares `Criterion: <name>` with the goldens pasted verbatim, and the guide instructs
   the DISPATCHING AGENT's fold procedure: a finding row lacking a class from the declared
   enum is not admitted to the findings ledger. Stated honestly per the corpus's own
   doctrine: on a prose route this is steering, not control — the fold is performed by an
   agent following the guide, and nothing structural refuses for it until stage 6's
   schema-backed channel exists. The guide therefore carries the fold rule as an explicit
   numbered procedure (part of build step 2), and the Done-when's dogfood packet is the
   test: its findings table must show the class column populated from the declared enum,
   with any class-less row recorded as refused. (Amended per PR review: the launcher's
   `--fold-receipts` folds receipt METADATA and is not this fold; v1 adds no code.)
3. **The launcher registry** (deferred, separate approval): the seam exists —
   `severity_emits` → `{severities}` slot → review-matrix golden — and a `{criterion}` slot
   would mirror it. GPT's contract design is the seed: compiled criterion-specific result
   schema (`--output-schema` / `--json-schema` per host, re-probed at build time), request/
   result digests bound into receipts, adapter offers declaring which output contracts they
   enforce, no-schema routes disclosed rather than credited. Deferred because a criterion
   is per-review while the launcher renders per-launch, and because the receipt subsystem
   just stabilized — growing its golden surface immediately is the wrong order.

## 6. Migration *(both drafts converged; GPT's two-track note kept)*

The next review packet this repo dispatches declares its criterion by name (a harness round
declares `AI harness`; a general launcher-behavior round does not auto-inherit it — it
declares a **task-local criterion** written in the packet itself, conforming to the §3
schema and labeled task-local, until repeated use earns a catalog entry (amended per PR
review — the catalog has no CLI/launcher entry, so without this rule that migration
sentence would be unsatisfiable); a mixed packet is split rather than running two observers
in one trajectory). Prior round records
are dated history, untouched; re-classification applies only to findings still open when a
criterion changes, and pre/post-switch counts are never spliced — which is also GPT's
correction to the evidence itself: the origin's series and this repo's (8→9→10→5→12,
13→6→6→3→1→2→3) are two populations and stay two.

## 7. Build order — five steps, one PR, approval first

1. **Lexicon**: graph entry → `emit-lexicon.py` → `check-lexicon.py`. *Control*: hand-edit
   the emitted LEXICON.md → `--check` fails; revert.
2. **The guide** (+ko), classified in `compose/domains.json`. *Controls*: manifest row
   absent → `check-domains.py` fails; a planted origin-repo path in the body →
   `check-package.sh` shipped-prose leg fails by name; remove both plants. **The catalog's
   own admission rules are review obligations of this step's PR** (amended per PR review —
   v1 ships no machine-readable catalog for a gate to parse): the PR body counts each
   entry's goldens (≥2+/≥2−/≥1 boundary), names each golden's provenance label, and asserts
   the AI-harness entry present; an entry below bar is dropped WITH its migration
   sentences, never shipped thin. Mechanizing this audit is part of the deferred stage 6.
3. **Mirrors**: `emit-mirrors.py`; parity. *Control*: hand-edit one emitted codex line →
   parity fails; revert.
4. **Pointer edits** in the two parent guides; re-emit; parity. *Control*: if a touched
   phrase is pinned by the anchor-phrase list, edit one side only → parity fails → complete.
5. **Provenance**: the decision record closing (b), (c), and (d-GPT-in-v1) is recorded
   WITH this design (amended per PR review — a record that waits for an implementation
   that may never come leaves the closure unprovenanced): D-20260818 in the ledger on this
   branch. What remains for this step at build time is only the post-build dated record.
   *Control*: `record-decision.py --check`; an empty `--closed` is refused by the tool.
6. *(Deferred, separate design+approval)*: the launcher `{criterion}` slot per §5.3.

**Done-when**: parity green from the clone; the guide in all four trees; a clone deploy
routes `review-request.md` → the new guide; the decision record exists; the next dispatched
packet names its criterion. Every green preceded by its control having been seen to fail.

## 8. Open questions (neutral, from both drafts)

1. **Redistribution of origin examples** (GPT): may the source repo's concrete goldens ship
   verbatim, or must each be normalized into a self-contained fixture? (v1 rewrites any
   golden whose why needs the origin's code; the licensing question is the user's.)
2. **Per-repo extension** (Claude): org/repo-local criteria via a user-owned file
   (precedent: `review-methods.local.toml`) or via the repo's AGENTS.md (precedent:
   repo-charter teaches, never fills)? The first real second-repo criterion decides.
3. **Harness reviews as a separate track**: 4-C's stop unit is "this run's signals", not "a
   surface reaches zero"; whether they may never share a loop with surface reviews is left
   to evidence from the first declared-criterion loops.
4. **Mid-loop switching cost**: the re-classification rule ships; whether a switch demands
   a decision record in the dispatching repo is open.
5. **Ultracode's channel** (GPT): can that workflow route preserve strict structured output,
   or does it stay a rank-2 prose route until a constrained submit path exists?
