# Repository, personal and Team composition

The user selected repository → personal → Team as the default content application
order, with separate Instructions, knowledge and decision material at each scope.
This is a direction change from the previous no-default-precedence design. The
purpose is efficient local adaptation, not enforcement of uniform Team work content.
The canonical AGENTS purpose and its README projection now state that purpose.

The complete successor SSOT and engineering projections replace the conflicting
rules together. They distinguish nine original collection positions from the
derived effective environment. Applicable overlapping units follow the default
scope order; unrelated lower content remains. The source's owner, version, factual
qualifications and decision history survive application selection.

Own repository/personal content may replace Team defaults without a Team vote,
waiver, readoption or research-only restriction. A shadowed lower Team mandatory
flag or unavailable body cannot become a startup gate. Actual protected-byte
access/disclosure and Team source/grant/shared-state changes retain their narrow
resource authorizations. Those checks cannot be repurposed as Team content control.

Explicit keys/references support bounded automatic selection. Ordinary unkeyed
prose retains its separate layers and the same application order; it does not
require a new schema questionnaire or revert to no precedence. No universal
semantic merge engine or new global policy service is introduced.

Disabling a source/position excludes that selection until reenabled; other layers
can still supply the concern. Absence permits inheritance, while a missing/denied
selected winning required unit remains a gap. Same-layer ambiguity is resolved by
an explicit choice or stays qualified for its affected action/query. No task intent
is inferred on entry. Existing session pins remain attributable and unchanged.

## Corrections found in cross-review

The old Greenfield paragraph, P19 completion rule and CAP-08 oracle still described
local candidates as research-only. All three now allow ordinary permitted local
work while retaining actual candidate/unadopted provenance. Source adoption and
shared publication remain separate facts.

The review also preserved exact prepared/delivered snapshots: forbidding a derived
view from becoming an editable original must not forbid retaining rendered bytes
for session continuity. The work-progress writer/schema gap raised earlier remains
explicit in S14; this scope-composition change does not pretend to implement it.

## Evidence and limits

- The graph remains 25 nodes with seven existing acceptance obligations. P11 and
  M1 gain no Team runtime prerequisite. Nine CMP atoms are consumed by the existing
  personal, Team, operations and integrated obligations.
- The author evaluator's 9 positive and 78 negative controls pass. New controls
  reject a Team-first default, loss of separate roles, local Team-approval gates,
  shadowed-Team startup gates, an authoritative effective view, and loss of local
  adaptation acceptance evidence. This is an evidence-model test, not product code.
- The browser prototype passes 36 named assertions and 384 scenario/width/layout/
  appearance combinations without horizontal control clipping. It tests all three
  role winners, lower non-overlaps, no-repository fallback, explicit exclusion,
  lower Team denial/missing non-gates, required winner refusal, same-layer selection,
  protected context and immutable original/unknown-request behavior.
- The wide initial view is 593 pixels high with a reachable start action. At narrow
  widths, the scope rows stack with visible role labels instead of shrinking text.
  Pixel rendering does not establish actual terminal, IME or screen-reader support.
- The prototype uses fictional explicitly keyed units and local selection state.
  It does not prove database persistence, arbitrary prose interpretation, actual
  Team ACLs/publication, unkeyed model behavior or real host delivery. Those remain
  the corresponding P03/P06/P10/P11 and M1/M2/M3/P17 work and evidence.
