# Spec round 4 — closure

The fourth round under the invariants instrument, against the third revision
`…T2310--11ed16b--review-evidence-invariants.md`. Three code findings, and **all three
were real** — each reproduced with the reviewer's own mutation AND its control before
anything was changed, fixed where the invariant is owned, given an assertion in the check
the reviewer named as missing, and then proven by reverting that fix alone and watching
the assertion fail by name.

**The shape is round 3's own fixes, one side short.** Round 3 gave the controls snapshot
one validator with two readers. Round 4's three findings are what that left: the OTHER
half of the same declaration — `evidence` — still had its grammar written once per reader
and asked of neither side at the adjudicator; the MAP holding those declarations was
required to be a map on the evidence side and not on the controls side; and the fold's
marker comparison answered a question one word away from the one F2 asks. A validator
unified across the readers that were in front of you is not the same as a validator
covering the readers there are.

**A replacement can delete the bar it replaces.** The reviewer's F2 proposal was to
compare `{field_name in receipt}` *instead of* `bool(receipt.get(...))`. Taken literally,
a pass carrying `ordering_seed="seed"` and one carrying `""` both hold the key and fold
clean — which is precisely the bar round 22 bought, and the gate case asserting it would
have gone on passing while asserting a refusal nothing emits. The two questions are
different: reportedness is what the ADJUDICATOR reads (falsey is unreported, and the fold
must measure it the same way or the two disagree about what carrying a seed means), and
key presence is about what the fold EMITS, since it keeps the representative's key set
verbatim. So the door was added beside the old one rather than over it, each with its own
sentence and its own needle (`D-20260817-b442f5`). The revert proves the point rather
than the argument: with the reportedness door removed, `seed` against `""` folds.

## Where the fix departs from the reviewer's proposal, and why

- **#1 (L2/L3/V2).** The reviewer asked for the validator at the in-process row and the
  current declaration. Two additions. `parse_capability_offers` held a THIRD copy of the
  identical predicate, which is the shape this round is closing, so it is a caller too —
  as a predicate only, because its sentence is an authoring hint about omitting the key
  while the record readers' sentence is that the record is not one. Both messages are
  unchanged to the byte, so round 24's `not the list of field names its offer declared at
  launch` and round 3's marker needles keep their subjects. And the validator admits
  `list | tuple`: the two compared sides are different containers by construction — a
  record decodes to a list, `_row_from_v1` returns a tuple, a parsed offer is a tuple — so
  a rule admitting one of them could not be the one rule both readers ask. `str` is
  excluded by that same isinstance, or `"reached_seat"` would read as twelve field names.
  Recorded as `D-20260817-4c8325`.
- **#1, the marker.** "Marker check retained" is read as: `refuse_evidence_marker` stays at
  the two AUTHORING doors and stays a raise rather than a reason. It is not extended to
  the adjudicator, because nothing the verify path renders carries an evidence name into
  prose — `render_receipt_verdicts` prints verdicts, not the record — and folding a
  raising door into a reason-returning validator would rewrite two messages the gate
  asserts by name. Same decision record; its `reverses-if` is verification beginning to
  re-render the plan record.
- **#2 (F2).** Added beside rather than in place of, for the reason above
  (`D-20260817-b442f5`). The order is deliberate: reportedness first, so `seed` against
  omitted and `seed` against `""` keep firing on round 22's needle and only the pair the
  first door cannot see reaches the second.
- **#3 (V2/V7).** As proposed. `controls_snapshot_reason` stays the per-method validator;
  this is the map, and its sentence is deliberately distinct from the evidence map's — a
  caller holding no view of the descriptors and one holding no view of the offers are
  different failures.

## What the reverts taught

- **A revert can be caught by the door beside it, and that still fails by name.**
  Reverting the row-side evidence check leaves the empty-name case refused — by the
  REGISTRY side, whose needle the case does not carry. It fails as "refused by something
  other than its own door", which is the arrangement that keeps two doors on one validator
  separately provable. The same is true in the other direction: with the registry side
  reverted, the empty-name-in-the-declaration case is caught by the drift comparison and
  fails for naming the wrong door.
- **Reverting the element rule and reverting the call are different proofs.** Removing the
  `all(...)` half of `evidence_snapshot_reason` while leaving the container check makes the
  empty-name case ACCEPTED (`adjudicated anyway`) while the null cases stay refused — so
  the two halves of the grammar are covered independently, not as one.
- **A traceback needs its own arm or the control cannot fail by name.** Every case here
  that reverts into a `TypeError` — the null snapshot, the null declaration, both non-map
  registries — is caught by a broad `except` that names the traceback. Without it the
  reverted gate would abort the run instead of reporting a defect, which is a failure that
  names nothing and is exactly what V7 forbids of the command under test.
- **A behaviour-preserving half has no revert.** Restoring the inline shape rule in
  `_row_from_v1` and in `parse_capability_offers` leaves every check green, correctly:
  they were single-sourcing moves, and the only behavioural difference — a tuple admitted
  where TOML and JSON can produce only a list — is unreachable from either wire format.
  Recorded rather than claimed as proven.
