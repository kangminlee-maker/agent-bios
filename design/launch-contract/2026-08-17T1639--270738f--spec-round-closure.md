cannot say. Reading `packet_binding=none` and `grade_derivation=claimed` on every
verdict is the whole remedy for the halves of D4 and L5 that stay open, and the
gate asserts them on runs where they are true.

## Where the fix departs from the reviewer's proposal, and why

- **#1 (D4).** The packet arrives as its own `--packet` option rather than as a
  third value of `--verify-receipts`. argparse cannot spell "two or three"
  honestly — `nargs='+'` with a two-name metavar renders `PLAN [RECEIPTS ...]`,
  and a three-name metavar raises while formatting usage — and the two artifacts
  already there are required while this one is not. The file has the precedent one
  option over: `--evidence` is valid only with `--emit-receipt`, enforced in
  `parse_args`. Recorded as `D-20260817-c3a9f0`. The retention story stays Q3 and is
  not decided here; what IS decided is that a verdict reached without the packet
  says so.
- **#4 (L5).** Left unenforced with a rendered disclosure, and the reason is that
  both closures were measured and both refused. An **optional** main-seat field is a
  guard defeated by an absence — omit it and the grade is unchecked again, which is
  the shape this whole spec exists to close. A **required** one refuses every record
  written before it; that is the `controls`/`evidence` precedent, but it is also
  exactly the schema evolution the spec holds open as Q2, and taking it here would
  foreclose Q2's options. A third candidate — "two rows on one seat must carry one
  grade" — is **unsound**: `below_review_floor` reads the binding's `tier`, and two
  rows can differ there while naming the same provider, model and effort, so the
  rule would refuse a legitimate plan. Recorded as `D-20260817-47c998`.
- **#9 (L3).** Two departures, both recorded as `D-20260817-c54fa5`. The dropped
  row's attempted seat is restated in `detail` rather than deleted, because what
  the operator most needs from that row is which reviewer they lost; the golden
  re-capture is exactly and only that change. And `GRADE_NOT_REVIEW` stays
  admissible on a dropped row — the refusal is scoped to `GRADE_ORDER` members —
  because it is the documented exclusion marker for an unattested mechanism rather
  than a rung, and the **deployed** corpus guide names it in as many words. Nulling
  it would leave shipped prose describing a value the code no longer emits, which
  is a payload change this round has no mandate for.
- **#7 (S5).** The projection comparison is the **active host's only**. The obvious
  inactive-host bar — what the source preset projects there — is not a bar at all,
  and `preset_save`'s own subject proves it: that check customizes `main_tier` to
  `workhorse` on a codex plan, and holding claude to the source refused the save by
  name. A host-independent customization is supposed to move every host's
  projection. Nothing inside `save_preset` can construct "this customized setup, on
  the other host" without re-deriving the renderer, so there is no second
  projection to compare against; what an inactive host is owed instead is that the
  preset still **builds** there, which is enforced. Recorded as `D-20260817-57c57c`.

## What the reverts taught

- **A new door can silence an old control.** The existing "dropped row carrying a
  controls snapshot" case mutated only `status`, so the seat door added beside it
  fired first and that case began reporting a neighbour's message while asserting
  its own needle was absent. It now nulls the seat and grade in the same mutation,
  and the two new doors have cases of their own. This is the round-24 lesson
  arriving from the other direction: there, a shared needle graded every door;
  here, a new door stole an old one's subject.
- **A blocked destination is not a control for atomic publishing.** The first D8
  control planted a directory at the receipt's final name — and both designs fail
  identically at `open`, so the control survived a faithful revert. It only
  discriminates when the failure is injected at the **publish** step, because an
  emitter that writes the final name directly never calls `os.replace` and simply
  succeeds. Written that way, it fails on revert.
- **A fixture can be the thing that hides a finding.** `launcher_receipts` built
  every `passes` list disjoint from its own `result_sha256`, so the D7 tie between
  primary and passes had no subject in the check at all — the fixture was, in the
  precise sense, the reason nobody could see it. Two doors' controls
  (`duplicate-dispatch`, `no-ordering-seed`) had also been depending on the panel
  receipt being ACCEPTED, and went quiet the moment it stopped being.
- **Refusing earlier can leave a later guard with no subject.** Closing `panel` and
  repeated ids at parse made the adjudicator's own duplicate-row guard unreachable
  through a plan file — and a guard whose only proof is another guard is untested.
  It moved to an in-process subject, where reports really are constructed
  (`check_adapter_command` builds one), with a distinct-ids positive control beside
  it.

## Spec amendments proposed

The reviewer raised two specification defects. They are **not** applied here — the
spec is a dated record and the main session owns its revision. Exact replacement
sentences:

**1. D2's producer-authorship clause is not artifact-falsifiable.** In §2,
Dispatch-time, D2 currently reads:

> **D2 — Fresh dispatch identity.** `dispatch_id` non-empty, launcher-generated
> (uuid4 in `emit_receipt_command`, never adapter-chosen), ≠ `main_dispatch_id`,
> never reused across credited receipts — uniqueness judged over every id visible
> at the phase **[partially unenforced: per-method only today; global uniqueness
> across methods, and pass-id visibility after the lossy fold, are Q5 — GPT]**.

Replace with:

> **D2 — Fresh dispatch identity.** `dispatch_id` non-empty, ≠ `main_dispatch_id`,
> and never reused across the credited receipts visible at the phase. That an id
> was launcher-generated rather than adapter-chosen is a PRODUCER OBLIGATION
> (`emit_receipt_command` owns uuid4, and an adapter binds to the contract by
> calling it) and not a property of the artifact: two otherwise identical receipts
> cannot be told apart on it, which is the same limit §0's trust boundary draws.
> Verified uniqueness is therefore per phase — across methods within one bundle,
> and across the raw inputs of one fold — and the folded record does not preserve
> per-pass ids, so cross-method uniqueness has no post-fold subject (Q5).

**2. The "artifacts alone" preamble is too strong for D8 and S5.** In §2 the
preamble currently reads:

> Each is falsifiable: the violation decidable from the artifacts alone.

Replace with:

> Each is falsifiable: the violation decidable from the artifacts, plus — for the
> clauses that are about an INTERLEAVING rather than a state — a bounded runtime
> probe that injects a failure at a named step. D8's "a concurrent reader observes
> no file or one parseable receipt" and S5's "readers observe the old or the
> complete new file, and a lost concurrent save" are temporal properties: a final
> receipt or preset cannot say what another reader saw. Their artifact-decidable
> residue is the POST-FAILURE state — no partial file and no leftover temporary —
> and that is what a control asserts.

## Refuted pre-registration, confirmed

Pre-registration #4's visible half — a dispatch id reused across two methods in one

that the fold then discards) remains void: the post-fold behaviour is byte-identical
either way, which is Q5's subject and not a defect.
